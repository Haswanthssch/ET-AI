 Enterprise Data Management Solution 
 
The purpose of this document is to provide questions and answers regarding this RFI. The following 
Q&As are current as of 12/8/2025: 
 
Q1: Can companies from Outside USA apply for this? (from India or Canada) 
A1: There is no limitation, however we will expect any partner to comply with our 
Information, Privacy, and Security Agreement (IPSA) section 5.c: 
 Use, Storage, or Access to, City Data. C. Unless specifically authorized in writing by City, 
Consultant shall not (i) access, store, process, transmit, or create City Data at locations 
outside the fifty (50) United States of America; (ii) permit viewing access to City Data by 
Consultant or any of its agents (including any subconsultants) or any other person outside 
the fifty (50) United States of America through any screen sharing technology such as 
Remote Desktop Protocol or VMware Remote Console (“VMRC” ), or other current or future 
protocols designed to provide similar functionality; or (iii) provide City Data received from, 
created, or received by Vendor on behalf of City to any employee or agent, including a 
subconsultant, if such employee, agent, or subconsultant receives, processes, or 
otherwise has access to such City Data outside of the fifty (50) United States of America. 
The prohibitions set forth in this Section 5.c apply not only to Consultant’s data center 
locations and personnel primarily involved with providing the contracted Services, but 
equally to any and all data centers and personnel used for resilience or redundancy, 
backups, log storage, after-hours support, and any downstream partners that may access, 
store, process, transmit, or create City Data.

Q10: What are the top three business problems or pain points the City expects this platform to 
solve within the first 12–18 months? 
A10: 1) Collection of disparate data from multiple sources into a common repository in 
order to better utilize the data we have, 2) create a platform for future data collection, 3) 
create a process/template for data collection in the future so that new sources can be 
added to existing stores 
Q11: How mature is the City’s current data governance framework, and what gaps in 
metadata, ownership, or lifecycle management are driving this initiative? 
A11: The City’s data governance framework is emerging but not yet mature. We have 
policies, roles, and standards in draft, but gaps in consistent metadata, clear data 
ownership, and lifecycle management—especially for data that lives outside enterprise 
systems—are driving the need for a more structured and scalable solution. 
Q12: For integrations with systems such as EnerGov, Dynamics 365, UKG, and ESRI, does the 
City anticipate real-time data exchange, scheduled syncs, or API-level orchestration? 
A12: The City primarily anticipates scheduled syncs for integrations with EnerGov, 
Dynamics 365, UKG, and ESRI. While real-time exchange is not a core requirement, we do 
expect reliable, repeatable API-level integration patterns that support our enterprise data 
lake and reporting workflows. 
Q13: What level of departmental autonomy does the City envision—will departments build 
their own forms/workflows, or will configuration be managed centrally by IT?

A13: We envision a hybrid model: departments should have the autonomy to build simple 
forms and workflows, but within a centrally governed framework. TIS will manage the 
platform configuration, standards, and data structures to ensure quality, security, and 
alignment with our data lake and Power BI processes 
Q14: What challenges has the City encountered with Power BI that the new platform should 
specifically address (e.g., data refresh delays, access control, accuracy, dataset ownership)? 
A14: Power BI itself is not the issue—our challenges arise when data isn’t stored in an 
enterprise system. Today, many datasets live in personal drives, OneDrive, SharePoint, or 
ad-hoc spreadsheets, which creates accuracy, access, and refresh risks. We need a 
platform that centralizes and manages this non-enterprise data so it can flow into our 
governed data lake and be reported through our existing enterprise Power BI framework 
Q15: How does the City plan to measure success for this project? Are there specific adoption, 
performance, or governance outcomes that would define a successful implementation? 
A15: We will consider the project successful when departments consistently use the 
platform instead of personal files, data is centrally managed with clear ownership and 
metadata, and reporting becomes more accurate and timelier. Strong adoption, 
streamlined workflows, and alignment with our enterprise data governance standards are 
key indicators of success.
